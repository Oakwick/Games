Line Clearance

Mark the cuts to give the line its clearance: choose which limb, where and how you cut each one. Every cut is judged against BS 3998 good practice.

The job

Tree A
Side trim: 4.5 m clearance

Sycamore on the left of the line. Cut back the side facing the line so that no vegetation is within 4.5 m (horizontally) of the nearest conductor.

Tree B
Restricted full crown reduction: 3.0 m clearance

Oak on the right of the line. Reduce the whole crown evenly so that it keeps its natural shape and nothing is within 3.0 m of any conductor.

The clearance zones aren't marked on site. Judge distances from the conductors yourself using the scale bar. Clearance is measured to the nearest vegetation, foliage included. Get as close to the target as you can. Falling short of it costs you heavily, and over-cutting costs you too.

    How scoring works

    Each tree is worth 500 points, so the maximum is 1000.

    Clearance200Full marks for hitting the target exactly (A: 4.5 m side, B: 3.0 m all round). Marks drop away the more you over-cut (none left at 1.5 m over). Falling short of the clearance at all halves your marks, and they're gone by 0.5 m short.
    Cut quality200Every cut is scored out of 100 against BS 3998 good practice (below), then averaged, with bigger cuts counting for more.
    Crown100Remove no more than 30% of the leaf-bearing material.

    Each cut (out of 100)

    • Cut Position (45)
    • Cut Angle (20)
    • Cut Proportions (15)
    • Wound Size (10)
    • Technique (10)

    The clearance zones aren't drawn while you work, so judge them from the conductors and the scale bar. You'll see them, and your actual clearances, on the results screen. Equal scores are split by the faster time.
